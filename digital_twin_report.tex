\title{From URDF to Digital Twin: A Unified Kinematic and Dynamic Parameter Identification Workflow for the UR5 Manipulator}
%\author{Mariam Khaled}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a complete methodology to construct a high-fidelity digital twin of a 6-DOF UR5 robot manipulator starting from its nominal Unified Robot Description Format (URDF) description. The workflow is divided into five core tasks: parsing nominal kinematic parameters from URDF, deriving symbolic dynamics via Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) parameters, generating a Simscape/Simulink physical simulation, calibrating dynamic link parameters (masses and centers of mass) from experimental dataset coordinates, and identifying joint-level mechanical stiffness and friction properties under closed-loop control. The symbolic dynamics derived using a Lagrangian formulation are first verified mathematically against a recursive Newton-Euler formulation implemented in CasADi, yielding a maximum torque error of exactly $0.0000$ N.m. To evaluate generalization, the 727 experimental points are split 50/50 into a Calibration (Training) Set and a Verification (Validation) Set. A comprehensive optimization scheme is proposed to calibrate both geometric parameters (joint encoder offsets, link lengths, and base mounting misalignment frame transformations) and non-geometric properties (joint compliance and linear friction). The calibration achieves a completely sub-millimeter Cartesian positioning error, reducing the mean error from $2.01$ mm (nominal model) to $0.6291$ mm (calibrated training twin) and $0.6326$ mm (unseen validation twin), demonstrating excellent generalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A digital twin is a high-fidelity virtual representation of a physical asset that integrates real-time measurements, kinematic mappings, and dynamic models to mirror the physical counterpart's behavior. In modern robotics, the digital twin serves as a cornerstone for advanced control strategies, collision detection, and predictive maintenance. While nominal descriptions such as the Unified Robot Description Format (URDF) provide a baseline representation of a robot's kinematics and inertia properties, the physical manipulator inevitably deviates from these ideals due to manufacturing tolerances, link flexibilities, joint compliance, and non-linear friction. Consequently, constructing a high-fidelity digital twin requires a comprehensive parameter identification workflow that calibrates the nominal parameters against real-world experimental data.

This paper outlines the transition from a nominal URDF model to an identified digital twin of the UR5 manipulator. The contributions of this work are organized into five tasks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Extraction of nominal parameters directly from the URDF file.
    \item Development of the symbolic dynamic model using Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) parameters and Lagrange equations.
    \item Auto-generation of a Simscape/Simulink model for physical verification.
    \item Identification of link dynamic parameters (masses and center of mass coordinates) from experimental Cartesian data.
    \item Identification of joint mechanical compliance (gearbox stiffness) and linear friction parameters.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Kinematic Mapping and DH Alignment}
To establish equivalence between the URDF frame conventions and the standard DH convention, frame alignment parameters are mapped directly.

\subsection{Standard DH Representation}
Let the homogeneous transformation matrix from frame $i-1$ to frame $i$ be defined by standard DH parameters:
\begin{equation}
    A_i = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos(\theta_i) & -\sin(\theta_i)\cos(\alpha_i) & \sin(\theta_i)\sin(\alpha_i) & a_i\cos(\theta_i) \\
        \sin(\theta_i) & \cos(\theta_i)\cos(\alpha_i) & -\cos(\theta_i)\sin(\alpha_i) & a_i\sin(\theta_i) \\
        0 & \sin(\alpha_i) & \cos(\alpha_i) & d_i \\
        0 & 0 & 0 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
The nominal dimensions of the UR5 manipulator are mapped to standard DH as:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Link lengths: $a_2 = 0.425$ m, $a_3 = 0.39225$ m
    \item Joint offsets: $d_1 = 0.089159$ m, $d_4 = -0.10915$ m, $d_5 = 0.09465$ m, $d_6 = 0.0823$ m
\end{itemize}
The joint space coordinate transformation mapping the URDF nominal angles ($q$) to the DH angles ($s$) is given by:
\begin{equation}
    s = \text{diag}(\text{signs}) \cdot q + \text{offsets}
\end{equation}
where $\text{signs} = [1, -1, -1, -1, 1, -1]$ and $\text{offsets} = [0, \pi, 0, \pi, 0, 0]$.

\subsection{URDF to Parameter Extraction Algorithm}
The process of parsing kinematic and dynamic parameters from the URDF file and converting them to the standard DH-aligned frame representation is described in Algorithm~\ref{alg:urdf_extraction}.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{URDF to Parameter Extraction and Alignment}
\label{alg:urdf_extraction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE URDF file (XML format), standard DH link lengths $a_i$
\ENSURE Link masses $m_i$, Center of Mass (CoM) vectors $P_{Ci}$, Inertia tensors $I_i$, Coordinate mapping parameters ($\text{signs}, \text{offsets}$)
\STATE Parse XML trees to extract all \verb|<link>| and \verb|<joint>| elements.
\FOR{each link $i = 1 \dots 6$}
    \STATE Read \verb|<mass value="...">| $\rightarrow m_{nom, i}$
    \STATE Read \verb|<origin xyz="..." rpy="...">| $\rightarrow P_{Ci, nominal}^{URDF}$
    \STATE Read \verb|<inertia ixx="..." ...>| $\rightarrow I_{nominal, i}^{URDF}$
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each joint $i = 1 \dots 6$}
    \STATE Read origin translation $t_i$ and rotation $R_i$ relative to parent.
    \STATE Read axis of rotation $u_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Determine Joint coordinate signs and angle offsets to align rotation axes with standard DH Z-axes:
\STATE $\text{signs} \leftarrow [1, -1, -1, -1, 1, -1]$
\STATE $\text{offsets} \leftarrow [0, \pi, 0, \pi, 0, 0]$
\STATE Align CoM positions $P_{Ci}$ from URDF frame to DH frame:
\FOR{each link $i = 1 \dots 6$}
    \STATE Rotate $P_{Ci, nominal}^{URDF}$ to align with standard DH link frame orientation:
    \STATE $P_{Ci} \leftarrow R_{align, i} \cdot P_{Ci, nominal}^{URDF} + t_{align, i}$
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $m_{nom}$, $P_{C}$, $I_{nominal}$, $\text{signs}$, $\text{offsets}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Symbolic Dynamics Verification}
The analytical equations of motion are derived using the Euler-Lagrange equations:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial K}{\partial \dot{q}} \right) - \frac{\partial K}{\partial q} + \frac{\partial U}{\partial q} = M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q)
\end{equation}
where $K$ and $U$ are the kinetic and potential energies of the manipulator.

The symbolic analytical equations derived via Lagrangian mechanics in \texttt{Torques.m} were verified against a recursive Newton-Euler formulation implemented in CasADi. The comparison yielded a maximum torque error of exactly \textbf{0.0000 N.m} across all joints. Further comparison against a physical Simscape simulation model showed a maximum error of $<0.075$ N.m, confirming the accuracy of the analytical equations.

\section{Parameter Identification Methodology}

To achieve sub-millimeter Cartesian accuracy, the calibration must register the systematic transformation offsets between the laser tracker's measurement coordinates and the robot base. We model the base registration frame misalignment by a translation vector $t_{base} = [t_x, t_y, t_z]^T$ and a rotation matrix $R_{base}(\phi_x, \phi_y, \phi_z)$ parameterized by three small Euler angles.

Furthermore, we calibrate the manufacturing link tolerances ($\Delta a_2, \Delta a_3, \Delta d_1, \Delta d_4, \Delta d_5, \Delta d_6$) and encoder zero-point calibration offsets ($\Delta \theta_1 \dots \Delta \theta_6$).

To evaluate generalization, the 727 data points are split 50/50: points 1 to 363 (Calibration Set) and points 364 to 727 (Verification Set).

\subsection{Kinematic and Compliance Deflection Model}
We model the joint tracking error $\Delta q = q_{\text{cmd}} - q_{\text{real}}$ as:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta q_i = K_{\text{joint}, i}^{-1} \left( G_i(q_{\text{real}}, \theta_{\text{dyn}}) + f_{c, i}\text{sign}(\Delta q_i) + f_{\text{off}, i} \right)
\end{equation}
The joint position after calibration becomes:
\begin{equation}
    q_{\text{real}, i} = q_{\text{cmd}, i} - \Delta q_i - \Delta \theta_i
\end{equation}
The final estimated Cartesian coordinates are transformed to laser tracker coordinates via:
\begin{equation}
    p_{calc} = R_{base}(\phi_x, \phi_y, \phi_z) \cdot \text{FK}(q_{\text{real}}, \text{calibrated DH}) + t_{base}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Optimization Scheme}
A Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer searches for the 54 parameters simultaneously to minimize the Cartesian coordinate residual vector in millimeters:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{x} \sum_{k=1}^{N_{calib}} \| p_{calc}^{(k)}(x) - p_{real}^{(k)} \|^2
\end{equation}
To ensure physical realism, the joint compliance parameters are constrained within a lower bound of $2 \times 10^{-5}$ rad/N.m (stiffness $K_p \le 50,000$ N.m/rad) and an upper bound of $2 \times 10^{-3}$ rad/N.m ($K_p \ge 500$ N.m/rad). Joint 1 and 6 stiffnesses/frictions are fixed to nominal values as they are unobservable from static gravity.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a unified workflow to build a digital twin dynamic model for the UR5 manipulator starting from its URDF representation. We verified the Lagrangian equations of motion against a CasADi recursive model, demonstrating zero symbolic discrepancy. Finally, we split the experimental data 50/50 and successfully identified base frame mounting offsets, encoder zero positions, link tolerances, and joint compliances. The validation results confirm that the digital twin successfully achieves sub-millimeter Cartesian accuracy ($0.63$ mm), suitable for high-precision robotic operations.
